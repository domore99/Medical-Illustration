## What is Medical Illustration?

MedicalIllustration.app helps educators, students, researchers, clinicians, and health communicators draft **anatomy diagrams, physiology illustrations, surgical education visuals, and patient information graphics** using natural-language prompts. Users can specify the anatomy, viewing angle, labels, style, and audience, then review and refine the image before exporting it for further work.

It is intended to assist with **visual communication**, not to make medical decisions or replace qualified clinical review.

## How to use

1. Open [MedicalIllustration.app](https://medicalillustration.app/).
2. Specify **what** to show (an organ, process, procedure, or mechanism).
3. State **who** the image is for (students, clinicians, researchers, or patients).
4. Define the view, essential structures, labels, and preferred illustration style.
5. Generate a draft and check its accuracy against trusted anatomical or clinical references.
6. Refine the brief, download in an available format, and complete any clinical, publication, and accessibility review before distribution.

## Example prompt: labeled heart anatomy

```text
Create a clear medical education illustration of a frontal cutaway view of the
human heart for first-year medical students. Identify the right and left
atria, right and left ventricles, interventricular septum, aorta, pulmonary
trunk, superior vena cava, inferior vena cava, and major atrioventricular
valves. Use clean leader lines, readable English labels, and a neutral white
background. Emphasize overall spatial relationships; do not fabricate
anatomical features or display clinical measurements.
```

**Review checklist:** Are left and right orientations correct? Are the great vessels connected to the appropriate chambers? Are valve positions and labels plausible? Are simplified features clearly educational rather than patient-specific?

## Prompt-writing principles

- **Declare the audience and purpose:** a teaching slide needs different detail from a patient handout.
- **Request a precise view:** anterior, posterior, sagittal, coronal, cross-section, or exploded educational diagram.
- **List required structures:** do not depend on AI to guess the appropriate labels.
- **Use understandable language:** avoid dense jargon in patient-facing material.
- **Check every clinical statement:** a realistic-looking image can still contain errors.

## Medical safety and privacy

Generated illustrations may contain anatomical inaccuracies or misleading procedural details. They must be reviewed by appropriately qualified people before use in clinical or patient-facing contexts. Do not rely on generated images for diagnosis, treatment planning, consent, or procedural instruction. Do not upload identifiable patient information without a lawful basis, authorization, and appropriate safeguards.
